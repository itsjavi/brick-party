# Humor craft

How to make an item land. Each style in `packages/content/src/categories.ts` is a register with its own mechanism,
described below. The content guide (`packages/content/packs/README.md`) has the rules; this is the craft.

## Every item

- **One joke, turn last.** The surprising word or image goes at the end, where the reader trips on it.
- **Specific beats general.** "A Docker Compose file with 74 environment variables" lands; "a complicated setup"
  doesn't.
- **Short.** Cut every word the joke survives without.
- **Third idea.** The first idea is the one everyone writes. Keep going until it surprises you.
- **Original.** A premise already in the pack is spent, even in new words. Read the pack before writing.
- **Silent delivery.** The joke speaks for itself: no explanation, no "lol", no emoji.

## Registers

- **observational**: a truth everyone recognizes, said more precisely than anyone has said it before. Its moves:
  - *The self-image gap.* Someone's confident idea of themselves next to what everyone else plainly sees.
  - *Mundane stakes, total commitment.* A moved chair or a missing mug handled like a crisis.
  - *The flat aside.* A deadpan reaction line right after the absurd one, like a look at the camera.
- **satire**: pick a target (a practice, an industry, a company, a role, a machine) and push its own logic one step
  past where it already is. Aim up or sideways, at systems and roles, never at groups of people. Its moves:
  - *Every institution, slightly broken.* The plant, the school, the help desk, each run a little absurdly.
  - *The person against the system.* A simple want meets a system that makes it impossible.
  - *The petty ceremony.* Trivial achievements and rituals treated with full pomp: an award for most improved
    stapler use.
  - *Future tech that fixes nothing.* An amazing invention applied to the same old problem.
- **absurd**: an ordinary thing in the wrong place or job, played completely straight. Its moves:
  - *Machines with human vices.* Robots and appliances that are lazy, vain, greedy or petty.
  - *The official department of something silly.* A committee, ministry or form for a trivial thing, run seriously.
  - *The sign gag.* A label, a sign or a shop name carries the joke; in Skip Logic, it's the detail that makes the
    drawing.
- **unhinged**: extreme absurdity told deadpan. Its moves:
  - *Deadpan frame, impossible subject.* The survey voice stays serious while the subject is nonsense.
  - *Cosmic stakes, petty concerns.* The end of the universe gets handled like a calendar invite; a stolen stapler
    gets handled like a war.
  - *The trivial machine.* A device learns that its one tiny job is all it's for, and takes it badly.
  - *Sentient concepts.* Q3, the roadmap, Wednesday or gravity, with opinions, lawyers and calendars.
  - *Every version of you.* Parallel universes as a casual premise.
  - *The solemn technique.* A trivial move announced like a forbidden martial art: "Forbidden Technique: Reply-All."
  - *The swerve.* A tense moment derails into something unrelated, and nobody acknowledges it. Or it simply stops,
    as if halted for being too silly.
  - *Rigorous nonsense.* Flawless reasoning applied to a ridiculous premise, followed all the way.
  - *Denying the obvious.* A calm customer-service voice insists the plainly false is true.
  - *Points for everything.* A cosmic system scores everyday acts with absurd precision: minus 40 points for
    replying all.
  - *The perfect place, badly run.* An ideal world managed like a dysfunctional company.
  - *Ethics for trivia.* Grand moral questions applied to tiny choices, like who takes the last biscuit.
  - *The far future, same job.* A thousand years of progress, and work still has a standup.
  - *Today as a relic.* Ordinary present-day things puzzled over by a distant future.
  - *The sincere commercial.* A nonsensical product sold with total conviction: "Instant meeting powder: just add
    water."
  - *Escalation.* Each clause goes one notch further than the last.
  - *One gag.* The item ends before the joke gets old.
- **dark**: doom played for laughs: robot overlords, humans as batteries, the heat death of the universe, cartoon
  peril. The dread lands on machines, systems and the cosmos, and the violence stays cartoon.
- **anti**: the most literal, boring answer, delivered with full seriousness: "Rate the door. It says push, and it
  opens when you push." Works as seasoning, about one item in ten.
- **wordplay**: puns and double meanings in plain English that non-native speakers get too, like a hamburger menu
  that is an actual hamburger.

## Spicy

Spicy means punchy: a sharp roast (`satire`), doom played for laughs (`dark`) or extreme absurdity (`unhinged`). A
mild joke with an edge is still mild, so tag it mild. Push a spicy item until it's sharper, stranger or more
surprising than anything an HR newsletter would print, while staying inside the content guide's safety rules.

## Per game

- **Worst Feedback Ever:** an answer card is funny in most prompts, so it's a self-contained noun phrase. A prompt
  leaves room for many different funny answers.
- **Bot or Not:** the joke is the reveal: both questions side by side, next to everyone's answers. The Bot's
  question is close enough to blend in and off enough to be caught.
- **Survey Says:** a question splits the room, so predicting the team is a real guess. Labels and options carry
  much of the joke, and the last option is usually the punchline.
- **Skip Logic:** one creature or object doing one visible thing, drawable in ten seconds. The fun is how far the
  telephone chain drifts, so a vivid, simple image beats a clever sentence.
- **Make It Worse:** the survey is the straight man: the traps supply the chaos, so the form reads as a real one with
  a joke in its premise, intro and last options. Keep questions quick to scan, since players race through them.
